E-commerce: the problems behind the questions

Delivery uncertainty

Customers want a clear update. Your team keeps checking tracking pages and answering the same question.

  • Where is my order? What is your order reference? Please don’t share payment details. Prepared example; Connected shipment data.
  • My order says “processing”. Has it shipped? In this sample store’s approved guide, “processing” means the order is being prepared and has not yet been marked as dispatched. A shipment confirmation follows dispatch. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • My order was due yesterday. Is there a new update? What is your order reference? Prepared example; Connected shipment data.
  • My order is late and tracking hasn’t changed for three days. I’m sorry you are having to chase it. Please share the order reference and the delivery date you were given. Prepared example; Connected shipment data + human review.
  • I never received a tracking link. Can you check? I can collect your order reference and check an available approved order source. Prepared example; Connected order data + human review.
  • It says delivered, but I haven’t received anything. I’m sorry. Please share the order reference and check the safe location or reception listed in your delivery instructions, if applicable. Prepared example; Shipment data + human investigation.

Buying questions

Product details, suitability and availability questions interrupt your team and leave shoppers uncertain.

  • Is this bag waterproof or only water-resistant? The approved product description for the sample bag says water-resistant. It does not claim full waterproof protection, so I wouldn’t recommend treating those as the same thing. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Does the lamp include a bulb? The sample product listing states that the lamp includes the stand and shade, and the bulb is sold separately. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • I’m between two sizes. Which one should I choose? Which product are you considering, and which measurement is between sizes? Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Will this accessory definitely fit my older device? I can compare documented compatibility, but I need the exact accessory and device model. Prepared example; Knowledge + human review.
  • When will this sold-out colour be back? There is no confirmed restock date in this sample catalogue. I can explain the store’s published restock-notification process, but I shouldn’t invent a date. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Do you deliver to my postcode? Which postcode should be checked? Prepared example; Connected serviceability data.

Checkout & payments

Coupon confusion, failed checkouts and disputed charges need clear guidance and careful handling.

  • Why isn’t my discount code working? What message does checkout show? Please don’t share payment details. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Can I combine my welcome code with a sale offer? The sample store’s published promotion terms allow one promotion per order. The welcome code cannot be stacked with the sale discount. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Checkout said payment failed. Can I try again? First confirm whether an order was created or a debit is showing. If neither exists, follow the approved retry guidance. If money was deducted, avoid repeated attempts and have the payment reference reviewed. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Money left my account, but I don’t have an order. I can collect the transaction reference and the time of the attempt. Do not share your card number, PIN or one-time code. Prepared example; Payment and order records + human review.
  • I was charged twice for the same order. I’m sorry. Please share the order and transaction references through the agreed secure process; no card credentials are needed here. Prepared example; Payment records + human authority.
  • Is cash on delivery available for my order? The approved payment guide explains cash-on-delivery conditions. Order-specific availability must come from the eligible checkout or connected serviceability result; I can’t confirm it from a general policy alone. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.

Changes after ordering

Customers want to edit, cancel or update an order while the fulfilment window is moving.

  • I entered the wrong delivery address. Can you change it? I can collect the order reference and explain the change window. Please use the approved secure process for the replacement address. Prepared example; Order data + authorised order edit.
  • Can I change my address after dispatch? The sample policy does not allow a direct address edit after dispatch. Delivery support can review the available carrier options, but a reroute should not be promised without confirmation. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • How long do I have to cancel an order? The sample store allows cancellation only before the fulfilment cutoff stated in its approved policy. An actual cancellation requires a status check and confirmation; asking to cancel is not the same as a completed cancellation. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Please cancel my order before it ships. Please share the order reference. I can check an approved source or collect the request for the order owner. Prepared example; Order data + authorised cancellation.
  • Can I change the shirt from medium to large? Please share the order reference and the requested variant. I can explain the edit policy and collect the details. Prepared example; Stock and order data + authorised edit.
  • The phone number on my order is wrong. I can collect the order reference and explain the contact-update process. Please don’t share verification codes here. Prepared example; Verification + authorised account edit.

Parcel & product issues

Damaged, wrong or incomplete deliveries need evidence, investigation and an accountable next step.

  • How do I report an item that arrived damaged? The sample reporting guide asks for the order reference, the affected item and the required photos through the approved channel. That starts a review; it does not automatically approve a refund. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • My item arrived damaged. I want a refund. I’m sorry it arrived that way. I can collect the order reference, affected item and required evidence through the approved process. Prepared example; Evidence review + human authority.
  • What details do you need if I received the wrong product? The sample guide asks for your order reference, the item expected, the item received and the required evidence. Please submit those through the approved reporting channel. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • I ordered a blue shirt, but you sent a red one. I can help capture the mismatch. Please share the order reference and the required item or label evidence. Prepared example; Order records + evidence review.
  • Only one of the two items in my order arrived. Some orders may ship separately if your store’s policy and shipment records support that. Please share the order reference so the available records can be checked. Prepared example; Order and fulfilment records + human review.
  • The product stopped working after a week. What now? I can explain the approved warranty process and collect the model, order reference and issue details. Please follow documented safety instructions and avoid an unsupported repair. Prepared example; Warranty knowledge + human assessment.

Returns & refunds

Eligibility questions are repetitive; failed pickups and refund exceptions need someone to resolve the gap.

  • Can I return this if I opened the packaging? Which product category is it, and has the item been used? Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • I’m outside the return window, but the item is defective. I can explain the standard return window and collect the defect details. A fault or warranty concern may need a separate review. Prepared example; Return and warranty knowledge + human assessment.
  • I bought this on sale. Can I exchange the size? The sample sale policy permits size exchanges only for eligible products within its stated window. A replacement size also needs available stock; the policy alone does not reserve it. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • The size I need is sold out. Can you arrange another option? I can explain the standard exchange policy and collect the order and preferred alternative. Prepared example; Stock information + human review.
  • Nobody came to collect my return. I’m sorry. Please share the return reference and the pickup date you were given. Prepared example; Pickup records + authorised logistics follow-up.
  • My return was received, but the refund still hasn’t arrived. I can collect the return and order references and check available approved refund information. Prepared example; Return and payment records + human review.

Repeat orders & subscriptions

Customers ask about upcoming shipments, recurring charges and changes that must be confirmed before the next cycle.

  • When will my next subscription box ship? Please provide the subscription reference through the agreed verification process. Prepared example; Connected subscription data.
  • Will the next box cost the same as my first one? Check the approved subscription terms and the upcoming-cycle price in a verified source. An introductory offer may differ from later cycles; I shouldn’t assume the first price repeats. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Can I skip one month without ending my subscription? In this sample subscription policy, a skip is permitted before the cycle cutoff. The account action still needs to be completed through the authorised process and confirmed. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • Please skip my next subscription delivery. I can explain the cutoff and collect the subscription reference. A skip must be checked and confirmed in the subscription system. Prepared example; Subscription record + authorised account change.
  • I want to cancel before the next charge. I can explain the cancellation policy and collect the reference through the agreed verification process. Prepared example; Subscription and billing records + human authority.
  • You charged me after I cancelled my subscription. I’m sorry. I can collect the cancellation confirmation, subscription reference and charge reference without payment credentials. Prepared example; Cancellation and payment records + human review.

Complaints & ownership

Repeated contact and emotional complaints need continuity, priority rules and an accountable owner.

  • How can I follow up on an existing complaint? Use the approved follow-up route and include your existing case reference. Keeping the same reference helps the owner find earlier details rather than opening an unrelated report. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • This is my third time contacting you. Nobody has fixed it. I’m sorry you have had to repeat this. Please share the earlier case reference and what is still unresolved. Prepared example; Case history + human ownership.
  • What are the ways to reach your support team? The approved contact guide lists the available channels and human-support coverage. I can explain those options without claiming a person is available outside the agreed hours. Prepared example; Approved knowledge.
  • I don’t want more automated answers. I want a person. Understood. I can collect a short summary and use the agreed human-support route. Prepared example; Human coverage and escalation route.
  • You promised to call me yesterday. Nobody did. I’m sorry that commitment was missed. Please share the case reference and the update you were expecting. Prepared example; Case history + human ownership.
  • I know your policy, but I need an exception this time. I understand you are asking for something outside the standard policy. I can collect the reason and requested outcome for review. Prepared example; Approved policy + business authority.

Support workflows for your industry.

Novara combines AI answers from approved knowledge with accountable human support for exceptions. Enable JavaScript to try the conversations and build a support brief.

E-commerce & D2C

Order questions arrive all day. Missing parcels and refund disputes need more than another automated reply. Give both a clear path forward.

  • Order tracking: Explain delivery timelines and share status from a connected order source. When a person is needed: The status is missing, conflicting or outside the promised delivery window.
  • Delivered, but missing: Acknowledge the concern, collect the order reference and explain approved missing-delivery steps. When a person is needed: The customer disputes delivery or the initial checks do not resolve the issue.
  • Refund exception: Explain the return policy and collect the order details and required evidence. When a person is needed: Damage, eligibility or the requested remedy needs a person’s assessment.

SaaS & subscriptions

Make documented product help easy to find, while billing exceptions and technical failures reach someone who can take responsibility.

  • Getting started: Guide the user through approved onboarding documentation. When a person is needed: The steps fail, permissions are unclear or the setup needs account-specific changes.
  • Duplicate charge: Explain the billing policy and collect the invoice references through the agreed process. When a person is needed: A payment needs investigation, correction or a refund decision.
  • Access failure: Offer approved recovery guidance without requesting passwords or one-time codes. When a person is needed: Recovery fails repeatedly or access requires a secure account review.

Education & learning

Help learners and parents find routine answers, and give access problems, payment issues and complaints a considered human response.

  • Class information: Explain how to find schedules and joining instructions in approved sources. When a person is needed: The schedule conflicts or the learner’s joining details are unavailable.
  • Course access: Collect the course and payment reference and offer documented access checks. When a person is needed: Payment and enrolment records do not align or the access checks fail.
  • Parent concern: Acknowledge the concern and collect the earlier case references. When a person is needed: There is a repeated complaint or a sensitive learning-related concern.

Consumer services

Answer the everyday service questions quickly, then bring in a person when a booking change or disappointing experience needs judgement.

  • Before a booking: Explain published service details, preparation steps and booking policies. When a person is needed: The customer has a requirement outside the documented service scope.
  • Rescheduling: Explain the rescheduling policy and collect the requested date. When a person is needed: A booking needs changing or availability cannot be confirmed by an approved integration.
  • Service complaint: Acknowledge the missed service and collect the booking details. When a person is needed: A service failure requires investigation or a remedy outside the standard policy.

Marketplaces

Make buyer and seller guidance consistent. Give disputes and payment concerns a clear route to review without automating the decision.

  • Buyer and seller FAQs: Explain the approved reporting process, timeframes and required details. When a person is needed: The situation does not fit the documented process or the customer cannot proceed.
  • Item mismatch: Collect the order reference, description of the mismatch and required evidence. When a person is needed: The listing and delivered item are disputed or a remedy needs assessment.
  • Payment dispute: Explain the published payout policy and collect the transaction reference. When a person is needed: The payment status conflicts or the expected payout window has passed.

B2B & growing teams

Give repeat questions a reliable first response and important account issues a clear owner, without building a full internal support department.

  • Service questions: Explain documented onboarding steps, service scope and standard processes. When a person is needed: The request involves a custom commitment or an account-specific decision.
  • Account request: Collect the request and explain the approved account-change process. When a person is needed: The change requires verification, permission or action in a business system.
  • Repeated follow-up: Acknowledge the urgency and gather the earlier case details. When a person is needed: The issue is repeated, time-sensitive or has a stated business impact.
